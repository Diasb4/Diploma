# Evidence of Relevance: User Interviews

This document records how the team checks that the supervisor assignment problem is real and what users expect from the system. Its first part is the interview guide and the list of assumptions to test. Results are added after the interviews take place, and each finding is marked as verified or still an assumption.

## Goal

Find out how thesis supervisors are assigned at Astana IT University today, where the process fails for students, supervisors and the department, and which of our assumptions about the problem hold up.

## Respondents

We plan 3 to 5 conversations of about 20 to 30 minutes each:

| Group | Target number | Why this group |
|---|---|---|
| Final-year students | 2 or 3 | They choose or receive a supervisor and know where the process hurts them |
| Supervisor (faculty member) | 1 | Knows quotas, workload and how applications arrive |
| Department coordinator | 1 | Runs the allocation and handles conflicts and reassignments |

Respondents are recorded by role and an anonymous label (S1, S2, P1, C1). Names are not stored in the repository.

## Assumptions to test

These statements come from the project proposal. None of them is confirmed yet.

| ID | Assumption | How we check it |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | Students are often assigned to supervisors whose research does not match their topic | Ask students how well their supervisor's field fits the thesis |
| H2 | Some supervisors are overloaded while others have free capacity | Ask the supervisor and the coordinator how many students each takes and who refuses |
| H3 | Allocation is done by hand in forms and spreadsheets and takes the department weeks | Ask the coordinator to walk through the last cycle and name the tools and time spent |
| H4 | Students do not know which supervisor works on topics close to theirs | Ask students how they found candidates |
| H5 | The current process feels unclear or unfair to the people in it | Ask all groups how the decision is made and whether they can influence or appeal it |
| H6 | A ranked list of preferences plus a match score would be useful and acceptable | Show the idea at the end and note reactions |

## Interview guide

Ask open questions first and describe the project only at the end, so the answers are not shaped by our idea. Ask for the last real case rather than general opinions.

### Opening (all respondents)

1. What is your role, and how long have you been involved in thesis supervision at the university?
2. Tell me about the last time you took part in choosing or assigning a thesis supervisor. What happened, step by step?

### Students

3. How did you find out which supervisors exist and what they work on?
4. How did you decide whom to approach? What information was missing?
5. Did you end up with the supervisor you wanted? If not, what happened?
6. How close is your supervisor's research area to your thesis topic?
7. What would you change in the process if you could change one thing?

### Supervisor

3. How many students did you supervise last year, and who decided that number?
4. How do students reach you, and how do you decide whether to accept someone?
5. Have you taken students whose topic was far from your field? What was the result?
6. Which information about an applicant would help you decide?
7. What would you change in the process if you could change one thing?

### Department coordinator

3. Walk me through the last allocation cycle from the first announcement to the final list.
4. Which tools do you use, and how many hours or days does each stage take?
5. What conflicts or exceptions come up most often, and how are they resolved?
6. How do you check that no supervisor exceeds their quota?
7. What would you change in the process if you could change one thing?

### Closing (all respondents)

8. We are considering a system that suggests supervisors by topic similarity, lets students rank their choices and runs a fair allocation with quota limits. What do you think about it, and what worries you?
9. What would make you trust or distrust the result?
10. Is there anyone else we should talk to?

## How results will be recorded

For each interview we will add a short record under "Interview records" below with the date, the respondent label, the main facts, direct quotes (with permission) and the pain points they named. After all interviews we will fill in the validation matrix: every assumption H1 to H6 is marked as verified, partly verified or not verified, with a reference to the interviews that support it.
